Is there a graph of infinite chromatic number such that every finite subgraph on $n$ vertices can be made bipartite by deleting at most $f(n)$ edges? #74 : [EHS82] [Er87] [Er90] [Er93,p.342] [Er94b] [Er95] [Er95d,p.62] [Er96] [Er97b] [Er97c] [Er97d] [Er97f] graph theory | chromatic number | cycles Conjectured by Erdős, Hajnal, and Szemerédi [EHS82] . Rödl [Ro82] has proved this for hypergraphs, and also proved there is such a graph (with chromatic number $\aleph_0$) if $f(n)=\epsilon n$ for any fixed constant $\epsilon>0$. It is open even for $f(n)=\sqrt{n}$. Erdős offered \$500 for a proof but only \$250 for a counterexample. This fails (even with $f(n)\gg n$) if the graph has chromatic number $\aleph_1$ (see [111] ). The analogous question with vertices instead of edges is true, and was proved by GPT 5.5 Pro (prompted by Chojecki) - see [750] .
