Every graph on $n$ vertices with at least $\frac{k-1}{2}n+1$ edges contains every tree on $k+1$ vertices. #548 : [Er64c] [Er74c,p.78] [Er78,p.30] [Er93,p.345] [Va99,3.55] graph theory A problem of Erdős and Sós, who also conjectured that every graph with at least\[\max\left( \binom{2k-1}{2}+1, (k-1)n-(k-1)^2+\binom{k-1}{2}+1\right)\]many edges contains every forest with $k$ edges. (Erdős and Gallai [ErGa59] proved that this is the threshold which guarantees containing $k$ independent edges.) In [Er78] Erdős says this is trivial for a star, and he and Gallai had proved it for a path. It can be easily proved by induction that every graph on $n$ vertices with at least $n(k-1)+1$ edges contains every tree on $k+1$ vertices. Brandt and Dobson [BrDo96] have proved this for graphs of girth at least $5$. Wang, Li, and Liu [WLL00] have proved this for graphs whose complements have girth at least $5$. Saclé and Woznik [SaWo97] have proved this for graphs which contain no cycles of length $4$. Yi and Li [YiLi04] have proved this for graphs whose complements contain no cycles of length $4$. Implies [547] and [557] . See also the entry in the graphs problem collection .
