Every connected graph on $n$ vertices can be partitioned into at most $\lceil n/2\rceil$ edge-disjoint paths. #583 : [Er71,p.101] graph theory A problem of Erdős and Gallai. If we drop the edge-disjoint condition then this conjecture was proved by Fan [Fa02] . There are many partial results towards this conjecture. We list some highlights below; $G$ is an arbitrary connected graph on $n$ vertices. Lovász [Lo68] proved that $G$ can be partitioned into at most $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ edge-disjoint paths and cycles, and hence into at most $n-1$ paths. This also implies the original conjecture if $G$ has at most one vertex of even degree. Chung [Ch78] proved that $G$ can be partitioned into at most $\lceil n/2\rceil$ edge-disjoint trees. Pyber [Py96] proved that $G$ can be covered by at most $n/2+O(n^{3/4})$ paths (that may not be edge-disjoint). Pyber [Py96] also proved the original conjecture if the subgraph induced by vertices of even degree is a forest. Bonamy and Perrett [BoPe19] proved the conjecture if $G$ has maximum degree $\leq 5$. Blanché, Bonamy, and Bonichon [BBB21] proved this conjecture if $G$ is planar. Anto and Basavaraju [AnBa23] proved the conjecture if $G$ is $2$-degenerate (every subgraph has a vertex of degree $\leq 2$). Chu, Fan, and Zhou [CFZ26] proved the conjecture if the subgraph induced by vertices of even degree is $K_m$ for some $m\leq 15$ and $n$ is odd. Dean and Kouider [DeKo00] prove that $\lceil \frac{2}{3}n\rceil$ edge-disjoint paths always suffice, even for disconnected graphs (for which this bound is optimal). The same bound was independently obtained by Yan in their 1998 PhD thesis . Hajós [Lo68] has conjectured that if $G$ has all degrees even then $G$ can be partitioned into at most $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ edge-disjoint cycles. See also [184] for an analogous problem decomposing into edges and cycles and [1017] for decomposing into complete graphs. See also the entry in the graphs problem collection .
