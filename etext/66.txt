The challenge is obtaining this with no exceptional set. Erdős believed the answer should be no. In [Er80] he explicitly asked whether there exists such a set where the limit is $1$ (and did not believe there existed such a sequence). Erdős and Sárközy proved that\[\frac{\lvert 1_A\ast 1_A(n)-\log n\rvert}{\sqrt{\log n}}\to 0\]is impossible. Erdős suggests it may even be true that the $\liminf$ and $\limsup$ of $1_A\ast 1_A(n)/\log n$ are always separated by some absolute constant. Horváth [Ho07] proved that\[\lvert 1_A\ast 1_A(n)-\log n\rvert \leq (1-\epsilon)\sqrt{\log n}\]cannot hold for all large $n$.
